🎯 What Amazon TPMs Are Actually Evaluated On

Amazon's interview loop is deeply tied to Leadership Principles. Every question — behavioral or technical — maps back to one or more LPs.

The Amazon TPM Loop (typically 5–6 rounds)

  • Phone Screen: LP-based behavioral + one technical/estimation question
  • Bar Raiser Round: Deep LP dive — this person holds veto power; they look for "raising the bar"
  • Technical Round: System design, architecture, technical trade-offs
  • Program Management Round: Ambiguity, cross-team delivery, risk, metrics
  • Product Sense Round: Customer obsession, prioritization, roadmap decisions
  • Leadership/Collaboration Round: Influence without authority, stakeholder management

What separates a TPM from a PM at Amazon

  • TPMs own technical delivery — you must understand engineering trade-offs, not just define features
  • You drive cross-team coordination with engineering, science, and ops without direct authority
  • You're expected to write narratives (Amazon's 6-pager culture) and challenge assumptions
  • Metrics ownership: you define success metrics AND own the dashboard

⭐ Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles

Memorize all 16. For every LP, prepare at least one strong STAR story from your own experience. Check off each one as you write your story.

💬 Behavioral Interviews — STAR Method

Amazon interviewers will follow up aggressively. Your answer should be specific, first-person ("I did", not "we did"), and data-driven.

S

Situation

Set the scene. Keep it brief — 1–2 sentences. What was the context and why did it matter?

T

Task

What was YOUR specific responsibility? What were the constraints or stakes?

A

Action

What did YOU do? Use "I", not "we". Be specific — what decisions did you make and why?

R

Result

Quantify the outcome. What was the impact? What did you learn? Would you do anything differently?

Amazon-specific behavioral tips

  • Interviewers take notes and compare answers panel-wide — be consistent across rounds
  • Prepare stories that demonstrate scale (Amazon loves big numbers) and speed
  • Don't share team wins. "We launched X" → "I identified the bottleneck, proposed the solution, aligned 4 teams, and we shipped 2 weeks early"
  • Always have a "failure" or "disagree and commit" story ready — they will ask
  • Avoid naming competitors negatively or badmouthing past managers

🏗️ System Design for TPMs

You won't write code, but you'll be expected to understand architecture, trade-offs, scalability, and failure modes — and to drive the design conversation.

TPM System Design Framework

1

Clarify Requirements

Functional vs non-functional. Ask about scale (DAU, QPS, data size), latency SLA, consistency needs.

2

Estimate Scale

Back-of-envelope: DAU × actions/day = QPS. Storage per record × rows = total storage. Don't skip this.

3

High-Level Architecture

Client → Load Balancer → Service layer → Data layer. Name AWS services (S3, DynamoDB, SQS, ElastiCache).

4

Deep Dive on Bottlenecks

Where does this break at 10x scale? Database read/write, hot partitions, cache invalidation, fan-out.

5

Trade-offs & Alternatives

SQL vs NoSQL, sync vs async, eventual vs strong consistency. Own your choices — no "it depends" without a follow-up.

6

Failure Modes & Monitoring

What fails and how? Circuit breakers, retry logic, dead letter queues. What metrics do you track?

📋 Program Management

TPMs at Amazon own delivery from kickoff to launch — across multiple teams, time zones, and ambiguous requirements. Show you can navigate chaos.

Amazon's "Working Backwards" Process (Know This Cold)

  • Step 1 — Press Release: Write the launch press release before any engineering begins. Forces clarity on customer value.
  • Step 2 — FAQ: Anticipate hard questions: Why now? What's the cost? What if it fails?
  • Step 3 — Visuals: Mock up user experience if relevant.
  • Step 4 — 6-Pager Narrative: Replace PowerPoint with a written narrative. First 20 min of every meeting = silent reading.
  • Why it matters in interviews: Frame your experiences using this language — "I wrote the PR/FAQ to align stakeholders before we committed engineering resources."

Prioritization Frameworks

PM

For roadmap and feature priority questions, use these:

  • RICE: Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort
  • MoSCoW: Must / Should / Could / Won't
  • Amazon's narrative: Always tie back to customer impact first, revenue second, technical debt third
  • Two-pizza rule: Know Amazon's philosophy on team size and ownership

Stakeholder & Escalation Management

PM

Common interview theme: "How did you handle a team that wasn't delivering?"

  • Identify blockers early — weekly written status updates
  • Escalate with data, not emotion; propose solutions, not just problems
  • Use "disagree and commit" when needed — document it
  • Distinguish "blocked" vs "behind" — different responses required

Risk Management

PM

Amazon values Dive Deep and Bias for Action together — know when to act despite uncertainty.

  • Maintain a RAID log (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies)
  • Pre-mortem before launch: what's the most likely way this fails?
  • Have a rollback plan — especially for customer-facing changes
  • Know the difference between reversible (Type 2) and irreversible (Type 1) decisions

Metrics & Launch Success

PM

Use AARM: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Monetization

  • Define leading vs lagging indicators upfront
  • Set up dashboards before launch, not after
  • Know your guardrail metrics (what you must NOT break)
  • Amazon loves COE (Correction of Error) docs — know how to write one

Ambiguity & Scope Creep

PM

Classic TPM scenario: "The scope keeps growing. What do you do?"

  • Restate the customer goal — scope creep is usually a symptom of unclear requirements
  • Use a scope document with explicit "out of scope" section, signed by stakeholders
  • Separate MVP from future phases explicitly
  • Quantify the cost of each scope addition in time and risk

Delivery at Amazon Scale

PM

Think in tenets, not just timelines.

  • Amazon moves at speed — bias for fast iteration over perfect planning
  • Understand service ownership and API contracts between teams
  • Operational readiness reviews (ORRs) before every major launch
  • Know when to use Single-Threaded Owner (STO) model

💡 Product Sense

Amazon's Customer Obsession LP anchors every product question. Start with the customer, work backward. Use the CIRCLES method for design questions.

CIRCLES Method (Product Design)

C

Comprehend the Situation

What is the product? Who's it for? Why are we building this?

I

Identify the Customer

Segment users. Pick a specific persona — don't try to serve everyone.

R

Report the Needs

What does this user need? Pain points, motivations, context of use.

C

Cut Through Prioritization

Rank features by user value × technical feasibility. Explain your reasoning.

L

List Solutions

Generate 3+ ideas. Don't anchor on the first one.

E

Evaluate Trade-offs

Pick the best solution and defend it. What's the risk? What do you sacrifice?

S

Summarize Your Recommendation

One crisp sentence: "I'd build X because it solves Y for Z segment with minimal technical risk."

Amazon.com mobile conversion dropped 15% — diagnose it

Funnel analysis, A/B test artifacts, seasonal baseline, external factors

Key Design Principles (from the guide)

  • Usefulness: Does it actually solve the stated problem?
  • Understandability: Can the target user figure it out without a manual?
  • Efficiency: Does it minimize effort for the user?
  • User Experience: Does it delight — or just work?
  • Communication: Does the product give the user feedback to avoid mistakes?
